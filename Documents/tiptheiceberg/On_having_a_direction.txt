Perell was fired from his first corporate job out of college, seven months after being hired. After he lost his first real-world professional opportunity, he started creating content, which has been the driving force behind his career growth.
The bulk of David’s success has come from his content creation habit.
Perell started producing a daily vlog, publishing for 114 consecutive days. The vlog never took off, but it served its purpose — it got him in the habit of regular content creation, and his output levels have only grown.
Perell’s a master at making connections between ideas that other people don’t see, thoughtfully and deeply exploring ideas, and searching for answers to tough questions.

Since he graduated college, Perell has published over 1000 pieces of content — vlogs, podcast episodes, and lots and lots of blog posts. He’s interviewed athletes, entrepreneurs, and authors, and the likes of Neil deGrasse Tyson and Seth Godin.
He has a monthly newsletter that he’s sent out every Monday for 75 consecutive weeks, and has over 11,000 subscribers.
In March of 2019, he launched his writing course, Write of Passage, which did six figures of sales in the weeks after launching.
The key to all of this comes back to his **content creation habit — which is systematized, streamlined, quality, and as regular as clockwork**.

Perell uses **Tiago Forte’s Second Brain** system as the basis for his work

"**I don’t actually read that much**. Because we don’t **save the things we read**, we end up forgetting how many ideas we’ve put on our brain. Don’t read more — instead, read smarter and better sources…
How much time did the author spend on this? If they didn’t spend a lot, it’s probably low-quality. Every **word per hour spent ratio** isn’t very good. A book that’s **someone’s life work, that was compressed into a small amount of pages**, that’s a great deal. That’s very efficient"

Default towards **reading things that will still be valuable in 10 years**

What you crave is to have a sense of direction a every moment. Not to be stressed, not disoriented, not bored. You never need "rest", it merely doesn't come natural to be focused at all times. That's ok, you can e.g. daydream in between pushups. Since your instinct is to remain engaged, ie avoid boredom, at the level of focus that comes most natural to your brain at any moment, you should strive for that engagement to be "productive" at all times i.e. research for content creation instead of playing video games for deep focus, planning your day or week / studying vocabulary instead of jerking off in periods of diffuse-mode thinking
I need parameters, I need to be channeled. I'm very happy becausethere are no choices. The loads leads in only one direction. I'm afraid that when I get to the choices, when the roads diverge, I will lose this focus.

Binging on videogames is a way to cope with a lack of direction. Same as people going to parties and trying every new drug there is. Lets have "fun".

Returning to any counter-productive, self-gratifying old routine (habit/lifestyle) is a symptom of addiction. It means it has become your default mode of experiencing reward.

You are bad at evaluating your own decisions regarding career, love, topic of study. Remember being enthusiastic about learning clojure, or negative about learning c++, and having your momentum reverse on both of those things as soon as you got to work on them?
We are emotional beings. Visit your mind palace on job satisfaction instead of searching for "your passion" in vain. Explore partners, follow your heart when not in doubt (it's futile to resist), but remember that real relationships are built with effort and honesty, not discovered.
